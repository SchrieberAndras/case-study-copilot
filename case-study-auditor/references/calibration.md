## Professor profiles

### COBE – Cases on Business Economics (Corvinus), course profile
Source: the course's case-solving guidance, summarised. This is the course's stated standard, not graded evidence; apply it to every COBE audit.
- Structure: Executive summary → Analysis (situation, environment, company → main challenge or opportunity) → Strategy (direction, creating and analysing alternatives) → Recommendation (pillars, illustrations) → Feasibility (implementation and timeline, risks and mitigation, soft and hard impacts, financial breakdown).
- Action titles: insightful, relevant, short, punchy; must pass "So what?".
- Storylining: McKinsey 12 (Analysis, Pillar 1, Pillar 2, Feasibility; 3 titles each), then storyboarding per section.
- Expected tools: competitor analysis with industry drivers and a competitive landscape, STP and personas, customer journey, adoption curve, MECE structuring, decision trees, mock-ups, best-practice use cases, bottom-up or top-down financials, KPI strategy maps, hard and soft KPIs.
- Presentation pitfalls they name: assuming the audience knows everything, no team introduction, too many messages per slide, rough transitions, bad proportions between analysis, recommendation and impact, fancy words.
- Q&A pitfalls they name: one-man show, no Q&A protocol, teammates contradicting each other, long answers, not answering the question, too many "may I add", phlegmatic answers, praising the jury's questions, impatience.
- Jury they describe: academic members (teacher, experienced case solver), case-company members (CEO, senior expert), industry experts (consultant, expert from another industry). Q&A personas: the Feedback-giver, the Skeptic, the Curious expert, the Forgetful juror, the Impact-focused juror.
